## 1. Russell Wilson / Broncos dead money (March 2024 release)

- VALUE: **$85 million total** dead money — an NFL record at the time. Split: **$53 million against the 2024 cap and $32 million against the 2025 cap.** Denver reached this split by declining to exercise Wilson's $22M guaranteed 2025 option bonus, which converted it to guaranteed salary that accelerated fully into 2024. The alternative post-June-1 style split the team passed on would have been **$35.4M (2024) / $49.6M (2025)** — the Broncos chose to absorb $17.6M more in 2024 to buy 2025 flexibility. The $53M 2024 charge was itself the largest single-season dead-cap charge in NFL history at the time.
- SOURCE: ESPN — https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/39654399/broncos-cut-russell-wilson-take-85m-dead-money-hit
- SOURCE (split detail): NBC Sports / ProFootballTalk — https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/broncos-release-russell-wilson-opt-for-53-million-cap-charge-in-2024
- SOURCE (corroboration): CBS Sports — https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/broncos-to-release-russell-wilson-reportedly-take-on-an-nfl-record-85-million-in-dead-cap
- SOURCE (corroboration): Denver Gazette — https://denvergazette.com/sports/denver-broncos/after-taking-53-million-salary-cap-hit-on-russell-wilson-for-2024-broncos-now-in/article_bee7ffb4-e272-11ee-9790-83e0b50b967b.html
- CONFIDENCE: **high** — sketched $53M/$32M is correct; the only discrepancy in circulation is the $35.4M/$49.6M figure, which is the hypothetical split Denver did *not* take and which some aggregators mistakenly report as the actual one.

## 2. Has the dead-money record been exceeded?

- VALUE: **Yes — but only after the 2025 season.** Through the entire 2025 season, Wilson's $85M remained the NFL record; no 2025 release or trade produced a larger charge. The record was broken on **March 9, 2026**, when the Miami Dolphins released QB Tua Tagovailoa, incurring **$99.2 million** in dead money (widely rounded to "$99 million"), spread across two cap years. Miami exercised Tagovailoa's 2026 option bonus to split it **$55.4M in 2026 / $43.8M in 2027**. Current record holder: **Tua Tagovailoa / Miami Dolphins, $99.2M.** Wilson's $85M is now #2 all time; Daniel Jones / NY Giants ($69.3M, 2024–25) is #3.
- SOURCE: CBS News Miami — https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/miami-dolphins-release-quarterback-tua-tagovailoa/
- SOURCE (corroboration): Washington Post — https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/nfl/2026/03/09/tua-tagovailoa-dolphins-released/ae11a32a-1bb9-11f1-a29c-fd43da9a479a_story.html
- SOURCE (all-time ranking): Bleacher Report, Mar. 8, 2026 — https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25403184-tua-tagovailoa-kyler-murray-and-biggest-dead-cap-hits-nfl-history
- CONFIDENCE: **high** — CBS explicitly states the hit "tops the previous record of $85 million that the Denver Broncos took for releasing Russell Wilson in 2024." Note the two framings: **through the 2025 season the answer is "no, Wilson still holds it"; as of today (July 2026) the answer is "yes, Tagovailoa holds it."** Pipeline fixtures should pin which asof-date they assume.

## 3. NFL salary cap ceiling by year

- VALUE:
  - **2011: $120.375 million** (often rounded to $120.0M in league tables)
  - **2013: $123.0 million**
  - **2020: $198.2 million**
  - **2024: $255.4 million**
  - **2025: $279.2 million**
- SOURCE (official league table, all five years): NFL Football Operations — https://operations.nfl.com/calendar-events/nfl-free-agency/nfl-salary-cap
- SOURCE (2011 exact $120.375M): NBC Sports / ProFootballTalk, "The CBA in a nutshell" — https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/the-cba-in-a-nutshell
- SOURCE (2013 and 2014): NFL.com — https://www.nfl.com/news/salary-cap-rise-to-133-million-shows-how-new-cba-is-working-0ap2000000331237
- SOURCE (2024): CBS Sports — https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/nfl-salary-cap-skyrockets-to-255-4-million-for-2024-season-up-record-30-6-million-from-2023/
- SOURCE (2025): NFL.com — https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-sets-salary-cap-at-279-2-million-per-team-for-2025-season
- CONFIDENCE: **high** — only wrinkle is 2011, where the official NFL Ops table rounds to "$120.0M" while contemporaneous reporting gives the precise **$120,375,000**. Use $120.375M as the exact value. For context, the 2026 cap is $301.2M and 2021 dipped to $182.5M due to COVID revenue loss.

## 4. 2007 New England Patriots — record and point differential

- VALUE: **16–0** regular season (first 16-0 season in NFL history). **589 points scored, 274 points allowed, differential +315** — the highest single-season point differential in NFL history at the time. The 589 points broke the previous record (1998 Vikings) by 33.
- SOURCE: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_New_England_Patriots_season
- SOURCE (points allowed, team-official): Patriots.com, "Inside the Numbers: The 2007 Season" — https://www.patriots.com/news/inside-the-numbers-the-2007-season-140016 (states 137 points allowed in each half, totaling 274)
- CONFIDENCE: **high** — 589 and +315 both stated directly; 274 confirmed independently by the Patriots' own half-by-half breakdown and consistent with 589 − 315. The team went 18–1 overall, losing Super Bowl XLII to the Giants 17–14.

## 5. 2008 Detroit Lions — regular-season record

- CONFIDENCE: **high** — no discrepancies in any source.

## 9. Patrick Mahomes — 2023 salary-cap hit

- VALUE: **$37,133,824** (~$37.1M), which was **16.9% of the 2023 salary cap**. Composition: $1,325,555 base salary + $12,858,269 prorated bonus + $22,400,000 roster bonus + $550,000 workout bonus.
- SOURCE: Over The Cap — https://overthecap.com/player/patrick-mahomes/5594
- CONFIDENCE: **medium** — the value is solid but **the sketch and much of the press disagree, because the number moved three times during 2023.** Coverage cites, in chronological order: **$49.293M** (originally scheduled 2023 cap hit, pre-restructure), **~$39.7M** (after the March 2023 restructure converting a $12M roster bonus to signing bonus, freeing $9.6M), and finally **$37.13M** (after the September 2023 reworking that added money in later years). Any pipeline test should specify *which* snapshot it means. The $37.13M figure is the settled end-of-year cap number and is internally consistent — its four components sum exactly. Contemporaneous press context: SI on the March restructure — https://www.si.com/nfl/2023/03/16/chiefs-restructure-patrick-mahomes-contract-create-cap-space ; Arrowhead Pride on the September reworking — https://www.arrowheadpride.com/2023/9/22/23882451/chiefs-salary-cap-patrick-mahomes-contract-details-more-money-now-added-flexibility

## 11. 2023 snap-count fact (100% of offensive snaps)

- VALUE: **Laken Tomlinson, LG, New York Jets — 1,101 offensive snaps, 100% of the team's offensive snaps in the 2023 regular season.** He was the only Jets player on either side of the ball to play 100% of his unit's snaps.
- SOURCE: NewYorkJets.com (team official) — https://www.newyorkjets.com/news/jets-snap-count-breakdown-2023-season-cj-mosley-quincy-williams
- CONFIDENCE: **high** — direct quote from the team's own snap-count breakdown: "Left Guard Laken Tomlinson led the way for the Jets as the only player on either side of the ball to play 100% of the snaps (1,101)." Note this is a *team-relative* 100%, not a league-leading raw total — the Jets ran an unusually high offensive snap volume in 2023, so 1,101 is a high absolute number as well. If the pipeline test needs a league-wide snap leaderboard rather than a single player, FantasyPros hosts a filterable 2023 table at https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/reports/snap-counts/?year=2023&show=perc (renders client-side, so it did not fetch cleanly here).

## 12. 2013 Seattle Seahawks — record and Russell Wilson's cap hit

- VALUE: **13–3** regular season; won Super Bowl XLVIII over Denver **43–8** (after beating New Orleans 23–15 in the divisional round and San Francisco 23–17 in the NFC Championship). **Russell Wilson's 2013 cap hit: $681,085**, roughly **0.56% of Seattle's salary cap** — the canonical illustration of the rookie-contract-QB competitive advantage. For reference his adjacent years were $544,868 (2012), $817,302 (2014), and $7,054,868 (2015).
- SOURCE (cap hit): Over The Cap — https://overthecap.com/player/russell-wilson/1569
- SOURCE (record and postseason): Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Seattle_Seahawks_season
- CONFIDENCE: **high** — the commonly cited $681,085 figure checks out exactly against Over The Cap's contract history; sketched value confirmed.
